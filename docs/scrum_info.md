# Information about the Scrum Process  
  
## Roles of Scrum  
   
### Product Owner (PO)  
  
 - ***Product Owner***, manages the ***Product Backlog***.  
 - Managing the Product Backlog Entails:  
	- Create, update, and prioritize backlog items (features, tasks, fixes)  
	- Keep the backlog clear, organized, and up-to-date  
 - Ensure value delivery  
  
### Scrum Master (SM)  
  
 - ***Organizes and Runs*** - the Sprint Planning, Daily Scrums,  
   Reviews, and Retrospectives  
 - ***Remove impediments*** - by helping team members solve blockers (technical,  
   communication, scheduling issues)  
 - ***Protects the team*** - by preventing scope creep (random new requests from the  
   client mid-sprint without planning)  
 - ***Improves the process*** - by gathering feedback in retrospectives and helps  
   implement better ways of working  
  
### Development Team  
  
 - Decides how tasks are done  
 - Deliver the sprint goal (Chosen from the product backlog during print planning)  
 - Testing and maintaining quality  
  
## Scrum System  
  
## Sprints  
  
In Scrum, the Sprint process is a short, fixed-length cycle (usually 1–4 weeks)  
where the team turns ideas into a usable product increment.  
  
### Sprint Process  
  
1. ***Sprint planning***  
	- PO updates and adds to Product Backlog, with prioritization  
	- Team meets privately to build the Sprint Backlog, i.e. decide what can be  
	  delivered in the sprint and how to do it  
	- Decide on what work to assign to each developer  
	- ***Think about:***  
		- Sprint Goal (the "why")  
		- Selected Product Backlog Items (the "what")  
		- Plan for delivery (the "how")  
  
2. ***Sprint Execution***  
	- Build and test the items in the Sprint Backlog  
	- The scope can be clarified during the sprint, but the sprint goal stays  
	  fixed  
  
3. Daily Scrum (Stand-up Meeting)  
	- 10-15 minutes every working day (Emphasis on being short)  
	- Inspect Progress towards the Sprint Goal and adapt the plan  
	- Focus:  
		- What was done since the last daily scrum  
		- What will be done before the next  
		- Any obstacles  
	- Not for problem solving, it is a mandatory meeting for progress management.  
	- Not a performance review a progress review.  
  
4. Sprint Review (with Entersekt)  
	- Inspect the increment and discuss the goals of the next sprint.  
	- ***Activities:***  
		- Present what was done in the last sprint  
		- Collaborate on what to do next  
		- Update the Product Backlog as needed  
	- ***Important:***  
		- Not a single speaker everybody talks  
		- No powerpoints, you demo code  
  
5. Sprint Retrospective (without Entersekt)  
	- Inspect how the last Sprint went (people, process, tools) and identify  
	  improvements  
	- Determine concrete actionable improvement items for the next sprint  
  
## Product and Sprint Backlog  
  
***Product Backlog:***  
 - **Prioritized** list of items you need to build the project.  
 - Described as user stories  
  
 ***Sprint Backlog:***  
 - List of items you want to implement for this sprint  
 - It comes from the top of the Product Backlog  
  
## Backlog Items Types (Issue or Work Item Types)  
  
A Succinct way to think of the differences in type is as follows:  
  
***Epic →*** “What big capability are we delivering?”  
  
***Story →*** “What piece of that capability delivers value to the user?”  
  
***Task →*** “What technical step do we take to build that piece?”  
  
### Epics  
  
A large body of work that represents a major feature area, capability, or goal.  
It’s too big to be completed in a single sprint and must be split into smaller  
deliverables.  
  
***Purpose:***  
 - Aligns development with business objectives.  
 - Organizes related stories under one umbrella.  
 - Lets stakeholders track progress at a higher level.  
  
***Duration:***  
 - Spans multiple sprints  
  
### Stories  
  
A small, specific requirement that delivers value to the end user.  
Written in the user's voice, often in this format:  
	"As a [type of user], I want [goal] so that [benefit]."  
  
**Important Note:**  
*The benefit is very important to be able to look back and see if it was achieved.  
So user stories should always include the why. Not just, "I want to see photos of  
the hotels"*  
  
***Purpose:***  
 - Focuses on user value, not just technical tasks.  
 - Often acts to split the Epic into sprint long actionable items.  
  
***Duration:***  
 - Fits entirely within one sprint.  
  
***Contains:***  
 - Acceptance Criteria  
 - Possibly several technical tasks  
  
### Task  
  
The smallest planned piece of work — an action item that a developer, tester, or  
designer can complete in a short time.  
  
***Purpose:***  
 - Breaks a story into actionable steps.  
 - Lets different team members work in parallel.  
  
***Duration:***  
 - Measured in hours, usually ≤ 1 day each.  
  
### Bug  
  
An issue in the system where the actual behavior doesn’t match the expected  
behavior.  
  
***Purpose:***  
 - Tracks quality issues separately from planned features.  
 - Allows teams to prioritize and fix problems.

## Rough Template to be used as applicable for Meeting minutes:  
  
Basic details – date, time, location, attendees (and absentees).  
  
Agenda items – the topics that were discussed.  
  
Decisions made – agreements, approvals, or resolutions reached.  
  
Actions to take – tasks assigned, deadlines, and the people responsible.  
  
Votes – if any formal voting happened, the results are noted.  
  
Other notes – relevant discussions or announcements.
